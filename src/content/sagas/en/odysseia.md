The Odyssey is not a poem about a war. By the opening of the first book the
war is over and has been lost by everyone, the winners included.

It is a poem about coming back. About a man Homer calls **πολύτροπος** — "of
many turns": the one who can twist out of anything, outthink anyone, find the
way around. For twenty years that gift saves his life, and for twenty years it
keeps him from reaching his house, because every victory of the mind comes
with a bill.

The ten stations below are the whole road: from the smoke over Troy to a
bowstring drawn in his own hall. Go down.
