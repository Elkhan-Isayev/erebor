At the edge of Ocean, where the sun never rises, he dug a pit and filled it
with sacrificial blood. The dead came up at the smell — all of them at once,
in a crowd — and Odysseus had to hold them off with his sword, because only
those he let near the blood could speak to him.

The prophet Tiresias named the condition of his return: do not touch the herds
of Helios. And he named the price if they were touched: Odysseus would still
reach Ithaca — alone, late, on someone else's ship, and he would find trouble
waiting in his house.

Then his mother came out of the dark. He had not known she was dead: it was
not illness that killed her but waiting. Three times he tried to hold her, and
three times his arms went through. Here too he met Achilles — the man who once
chose a short life and everlasting fame. Now, a king among the dead, he said
he would trade all of it for a hired hand's place on a poor man's farm, if it
meant being alive again.

Odysseus went down for a route. He came back up with a different piece of
knowledge: the glory he had chased under Troy is worth nothing here. Only the
going home is worth anything.
