For ten years the Achaeans stood under the walls of Ilion, and for ten years
the walls stood. Achilles died. Hector was killed and mourned. Ajax lost his
mind and threw himself on his own sword. The gates never opened.

Then Odysseus proposed the one thing nobody had proposed: stop fighting. An
enormous wooden horse abandoned on an empty beach looked like a parting gift
to the gods from a broken army on its way home. The Trojans pulled down part
of their own wall to drag it inside. That night the horse opened.

By morning nothing was left of Ilion but smoke. Twelve ships rode low with
plunder as they pulled away from the shore, and he was certain he would be
home within the month. He was wrong by ten years. The mind that took Troy did
not yet know that the gods send a separate bill for victories like that — and
that paying it would take longer than the war.
