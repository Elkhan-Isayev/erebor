A storm off Cape Malea tore the squadron off course and carried it nowhere in
particular for nine days. On the tenth a shore appeared: a quiet country where
people live on the flower of the lotus. The scouts were met without any malice
at all. They were simply offered food.

Whoever tasted the lotus neither died nor suffered. He stopped wanting to go
home. The memory of Ithaca was not erased — it merely lost its weight, the way
a stone does under water. Why row twenty years against the wind when you can
stay here on the warm sand and want nothing further?

Odysseus dragged his men back by force and lashed them under the rowing
benches while they wept and begged to be let go. Homer put this scene at the
very start of the road for a reason: almost everything that follows — Circe,
Calypso, seven years on an island where life is good — will be the same
temptation in new scenery. The temptation to stop going back.
