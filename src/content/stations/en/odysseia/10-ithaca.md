The Phaeacians brought him home asleep and left him on the beach with his
gifts beside him. He woke on Ithaca and did not recognise it: Athena had
covered the island in mist and turned Odysseus himself into an old beggar,
because walking into his own house was dangerous. For twenty years a hundred
and eight suitors had been sitting in it, eating through his herds and waiting
for Penelope to give in and choose a new king.

The first to know him was the dog Argos — abandoned, old, lying on a dung
heap. He lifted his head, recognised his master after twenty years, and died.
Odysseus walked past without a flicker: to weep would have given him away. The
second was his nurse Eurycleia, washing a beggar's feet, who found the scar
left by a boar's tusk; he got a hand over her mouth before she could cry out.

The third witness was the bow. Penelope brought out her husband's weapon and
announced a contest: she would marry whoever could string it and send an arrow
through twelve axe heads set in a row. The suitors warmed the horn at the fire
and rubbed it with fat; not one bent it halfway. Then the old beggar in the
corner asked for the bow. He looked it over the way a musician looks over an
instrument, and strung it without effort — and the first arrow went through
all twelve rings. With the second he killed the nearest suitor.

Even after the killing, Penelope did not throw herself at him. She ordered
their marriage bed carried out of the bedroom in front of him — and he
exploded: that bed cannot be carried anywhere, he cut it himself from a living
olive rooted in the ground and built the house around the trunk. Only then did
she weep. Twenty years she had held out by cunning — and the last test she set
was one she would not have passed herself.
