The cave stood empty, full of cheese and milk, and the men begged him to take
the haul and run. Odysseus wanted to wait for the owner — out of curiosity,
and out of a calculation about the gift owed to a guest. The owner turned out
to be Polyphemus: one-eyed, the height of a cliff, and he sealed the entrance
with a stone twenty men could not have shifted. Then he began eating them, two
at a time.

A sword solved nothing here: kill the Cyclops and everyone dies sealed in the
cave. Only wit was left. Odysseus poured him unmixed wine and gave his name as
**Οὖτις** — "Nobody". That night the glowing stake went into the single eye.
Neighbouring Cyclopes came running at the screaming: *who has hurt you?* —
*Nobody!* — and they went away again, deciding he was raving. In the morning
the blinded giant ran his hands over the backs of his sheep as he let the
flock out, and the men walked free, clamped to the wool of their bellies.

And then, safe, from the deck, Odysseus turned and shouted his real name
across the water. He needed the victory to have a name attached. Polyphemus
heard it — and called on his father. His father was Poseidon. From that second
the sea, the one road home, became the enemy. The mind that saved him is the
mind that doomed him.
