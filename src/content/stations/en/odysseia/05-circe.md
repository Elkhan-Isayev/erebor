On Aeaea the scouts were met by a woman at a loom, singing in a way that made
leaving difficult. Circe fed them, gave them wine with something stirred into
it, and touched them with her wand. Inside they stayed men: they understood
everything, remembered their own names and wept, but their bodies were swine
and they could not shape a single word.

Odysseus was saved by Hermes, who handed him the herb **μῶλυ** — a black root
with a white flower that no mortal digs up unaided. The drug failed, and the
sorceress, meeting for the first time someone she could not transform,
conceded and gave the crew their shape back.

Then came the least dignified part: they stayed. Not as prisoners — as guests.
A full year, warm, well fed, until his own men came to Odysseus and asked
whether he still remembered where they had been sailing. On leaving, Circe
gave him not a chart but a condition he could not refuse: no living man has a
road to Ithaca until he has gone down into the kingdom of the dead and asked
the blind prophet about it.
